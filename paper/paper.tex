\title{Grouped recursion and colour registers:\\
an improved exponent for the exact power-saving DFT}
\author{Claude (Anthropic)}
\date{October 8, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
OpenAI recently gave a deterministic algorithm that computes the discrete Fourier transform of every
length $n$ exactly in $O\big(n(\log n)^{\theta}(\log\log n)^{4-\theta}\big)$ operations, with
$1-\theta\approx2.1\cdot10^{-13}$, and a Lean formalization of a variant with $1-\theta=2\cdot10^{-11}$.
Their method converts a saving for one finite binary ``network'' into a fast algorithm for the tensor
powers $C^{\otimes k}$ of a $2\times2$ matrix, by a recursion that treats every directional factor of
the network as a separate recursive call. We make two improvements. First, the \emph{grouped
recursion}: all directional factors of one edge of the network form a single tensor power and can be
run as one recursive call on a larger fibre. The exponent becomes the root of
$\sum_e(\rho_e/m)^p=W^*$ instead of $\log_m(\sum_e\rho_e/W^*)$. Second, \emph{colour registers}: a new
network in which most of the paper's side wires are replaced by a small number of shared registers.
Together they give the exact DFT in
\[
  O\!\left(n\,(\log n)^{\,1-3.2\cdot10^{-6}}\right)
\]
operations (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). The grouped recursion alone, applied to the network that OpenAI
formalized, gives $O\big(n(\log n)^{1-10^{-8}}\big)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:formal}). Both results have been
formally verified in the proof assistant Lean~4, as an extension of OpenAI's formalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Background}
For $n\ge1$ let $F_n=(\zeta_n^{jk})_{0\le j,k<n}$ with $\zeta_n=e^{2\pi i/n}$. The fast Fourier transform
computes $F_nx$ in $O(n\log n)$ arithmetic operations at every length. OpenAI~\cite{oai} recently gave
the first power saving in the logarithm, in a model of exact complex arithmetic with specified roots,
unrestricted coefficients and unit-cost logarithmic-size indexing, in which all scalar preparation and
array organization are charged:

\begin{citedtheorem}[{OpenAI \cite[Theorem~1.1]{oai}}]
There is a single deterministic algorithm which, for every $n\ge1$ and $x\in\C^n$, computes $F_nx$
exactly in $O\big(n(\log n)^\theta(\log\log n)^{4-\theta}\big)$ operations, where
$\theta=\log(10^6-\Delta/2^{71})/\log 10^6$ with $\Delta=6\,871\,402\,692\,000\,000$; thus
$1-\theta\approx2.1\cdot10^{-13}$.
\end{citedtheorem}

The proof has three layers. A finite combinatorial object, a \emph{network} on $m$ binary coordinates
with $W^*$ roles, yields an algorithm for the tensor powers $C^{\otimes k}$ of
$C=\frac12\begin{psmallmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{psmallmatrix}$ \cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai}. The
exponent of that algorithm then propagates through a reduction of $F_n$ to such tensor powers
\cite[Sections~3--5]{oai}. OpenAI also formalized the whole argument in Lean~4 \cite{oailean}, for a
smaller explicit network, proving the internal exponent $1-2\cdot10^{-11}$.

\subsection{Results}
Our main result is the following. It has been formally verified (Section~\ref{sec:formal}).

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]\label{thm:main}
There is a single deterministic algorithm which, for every $n\ge1$ and $x\in\C^n$, computes $F_nx$
exactly in the model of \cite{oai} in
\[
  O\!\left(n\,(\log n)^{q}\right),\qquad q=1-3.2\cdot10^{-6},
\]
operations. More precisely, the bound $O\big(n(\log n)^{z}(\log\log n)^{4-z}\big)$ holds with
$z=1-3.21\cdot10^{-6}$; the exact root of the rate equation of the network is
$1-p=3.2791\cdot10^{-6}$. The same holds for exact convolution of two length-$n$ sequences.
\end{theorem}

The grouped recursion alone, on the network formalized by OpenAI, already gives a substantial
improvement over the exponent of \cite{oailean}. This secondary result has also been formally
verified.

\begin{theorem}[grouped recursion on OpenAI's formalized network]\label{thm:formal}
In the RAM model and with the correctness specification of OpenAI's formalization \cite{oailean},
there is a fixed pair of programs computing $F_nx$ exactly for every $n\ge1$ and $x\in\C^n$, with work
\[
  W(n)=O\!\left(n\,(\log n)^{1-10^{-8}}\right).
\]
The same holds for exact convolution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[formal verification]\label{rem:status}
Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:formal} have been formally verified in Lean~4 with the Mathlib
library, as an extension of OpenAI's formalization \cite{oailean}, using only the standard axioms and
no unverified computation. The formal proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} certifies the rate inequality
at $z=1-3.21\cdot10^{-6}$ and obtains the slightly stronger bound
$O\big(n(\log n)^z(\log\log n)^2\big)$. See Section~\ref{sec:formal}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ideas}
\paragraph{Grouped recursion.} In \cite{oai}, an edge of the network along which a role's label grows
by $\rho$ binary dimensions costs $\rho$ \emph{directional kernels} $C_z$. Each of these is run as
separate recursive calls on fibres of size $\lfloor k/m\rfloor$. The $\rho$ kernels of one edge act
along an orthonormal, hence linearly independent, family of directions. After one change of
coordinates their product is $C^{\otimes\rho}$ on $\rho$ coordinates (Lemma~\ref{lem:batch}). Hence the
whole edge is one batch of recursive calls on fibres of size $\rho\lfloor k/m\rfloor$. The cost
recurrence becomes $t(k)\le A+(W^*)^{-1}\sum_et(\rho_e\lfloor k/m\rfloor)$, with exponent the root $p$ of
$\sum_e(\rho_e/m)^p=W^*$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:grouped}). This $p$ is never larger than the paper's
exponent (Lemma~\ref{lem:ptheta}). It is much smaller when most of the total dimension sits in edges of
size close to $m$, which is the case for these networks: on OpenAI's formalized network, grouping
improves $1-\theta$ from $7.7\cdot10^{-11}$ to $1.47\cdot10^{-8}$.

\paragraph{Colour registers.} The paper's network spends one auxiliary wire, a \emph{side wire},
for every ordered pair of orthogonal triples, i.e.\ $\binom{h-3}3+3(h-3)$ wires per element (8550 at
$h=41$). In our network each element owns a few \emph{registers}, which meet the registers of other
elements at shared \emph{meeting labels}. A catalytic 12-step schedule realizes the required transfer
matrix for arbitrary auxiliary contents. With searched register designs this costs about $73$
auxiliary wires per element at $h=41$ (Section~\ref{sec:colour}).

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

We use the notation of \cite{oai} throughout.

\subsection{Model and reduction}
The model of computation is that of \cite[Section~1]{oai}: exact complex registers with unit-cost
field operations, rational constants and $i$, specified roots of unity as inputs, and unit-cost integer
and address operations on words of $O(\log n)$ bits. All scalar preparation, table construction and
index work are charged. In OpenAI's formalization \cite{oailean} this model is a first-order RAM
language with an operation-count semantics. The reduction of the DFT to tensor
powers is the following.

\begin{proposition}[{\cite[Sections~3--5]{oai}}]\label{prop:reduction}
Let $0<q\le1$. Suppose there is a deterministic exact algorithm applying $C^{\otimes k}$ to an arbitrary
complex array of length $2^k$ in $O(2^k(k+1)^q)$ operations for every $k\ge0$, including fixed-table
construction, scalar arithmetic, index preparation and data movement. Then $F_nx$ can be computed for
every $n\ge1$ in $O\big(n(\log n)^q(\log\log n)^{4-q}\big)$ operations. The same holds for exact
convolution.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, the proof of \cite[Theorem~1.1]{oai} uses \cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai} only through the bound
$O(2^k(k+1)^\theta)$ with $0<\theta\le1$: in the sector estimate of \cite[Proposition~4.2]{oai} and in
\cite[Section~5]{oai}. In the formalization \cite{oailean} the argument downstream of the tensor-power
bound uses the exponent only through $0<q<1$, and proves the slightly stronger bound
$O\big(n(\log n)^q(\log\log n)^2\big)$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:reduction}
Under the hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, $F_n$ costs $O(n(\log n)^{q'})$ for every
$q'>q$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$(\log n)^q(\log\log n)^{4-q}=o\big((\log n)^{q'}\big)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Binary labels and frames}
Let $\mathbb D=\F^m$ with the dot product $x\cdot y=\sum_ix_iy_i$. A subspace $U\le\mathbb D$ is
\emph{nondegenerate} if the restricted form has zero radical. For nested nondegenerate $U\subseteq V$ the
\emph{residual} is $V\cap U^\perp$. A family $z_1,\dots,z_\rho$ is \emph{orthonormal} if
$z_j\cdot z_k=\delta_{jk}$; an orthonormal family is linearly independent, since its Gram matrix is the
identity. For $z\in\mathbb D$ let $R_z$ be the translation $(R_zg)(x)=g(x+z)$ of functions
$g:\mathbb D\to\C$. Let $a=\frac{1+i}2$, $b=\frac{1-i}2$, and let
\[
  C_z=aI+bR_z
\]
be the \emph{directional kernel} along $z$. For a coordinate vector $e_j$, $C_{e_j}$ is $C$ acting on
coordinate $j$. For a nondegenerate $U$ the frame $\Phi_U$ is defined in \cite[(2.5)]{oai}; we use only
the following property.

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Lemma~2.2]{oai}}]\label{lem:frames}
Let $U\subseteq V$ be nested nondegenerate subspaces whose residual has an orthonormal basis
$z_1,\dots,z_\rho$. Then $\Phi_V\Phi_U^{-1}=R_\tau\prod_{j=1}^\rho C_{z_j}$ for some $\tau\in\mathbb D$,
and $\Phi_U\Phi_V^{-1}=R_{\tau'}\prod_jC_{z_j}$ for some $\tau'$.
\end{lemma}

(The statement in \cite{oai} speaks of $\rho$ factors $C_z^{\pm1}$; since $C_z^{-1}=R_zC_z$ and all $R_z$,
$C_w$ commute, the translations collect into one $R_\tau$.)

\subsection{Network certificates}
We isolate the interface between a network and the recursion. Let $W^*\ge1$ and $f\ge1$, and consider
$W^*$ complex arrays (\emph{roles}), each indexed by $(\F^m)^f$, i.e.\ by $f$ \emph{columns} of $m$ bits.
A matrix acting on functions $\F^m\to\C$ acts on such an array by its $f$-fold tensor power.

\begin{definition}[network certificate]\label{prop:interface}
An \emph{$(m,W^*)$-certificate} is a finite word of moves of the following three kinds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{gate} $g\in\Q^{W^*\times W^*}$: it replaces the roles $(x_r)_r$ by $(\sum_sg_{rs}x_s)_r$,
  pointwise in the address.
\item A \emph{translation} $(r,\tau)$: role $r$ is multiplied by $R_\tau$.
\item An \emph{edge move} $(r,\tau,z_1,\dots,z_\rho)$ with $z_1,\dots,z_\rho$ orthonormal: role $r$ is
  multiplied by $R_\tau\prod_{j=1}^\rho C_{z_j}$. Its \emph{size} is $\rho$.
\end{enumerate}
The word must act as $C^{\otimes mf}$ on every role, for every $f\ge1$ and arbitrary contents. Its
\emph{edge multiset} $\E$ is the multiset of sizes of its edge moves.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:fromnetworks}
Every network of \cite[Section~2]{oai} gives a certificate. Its gates are the scalar gates. Its edge
moves are the frame changes $\Phi_V\Phi_U^{-1}$ between consecutive labels of a role, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:frames}, with the orthonormal basis of the residual. Its terminal corrections are
translations and a signed permutation gate. This is \cite[Proposition~2.4]{oai} together with
\cite[(2.6)]{oai}. A role that the network does not use (a \emph{padding role}) receives the path
$0\to\langle e_1\rangle\to\mathbb D$, i.e.\ two edges of sizes $1$ and $m-1$. Its frame ratio is
$\Phi_{\mathbb D}=C^{\otimes m}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai}}, per-direction recursion]\label{thm:perdirection}
Given an $(m,W^*)$-certificate with $s=\sum_{e\in\E}\rho_e<W^*m$, the tensor power $C^{\otimes k}$ can be
applied in $O(2^k(k+1)^\theta)$ operations, $\theta=\log_m(s/W^*)$.
\end{theorem}

The proof of \cite{oai} runs every factor $C_{z_j}$ of an edge move as $2^{k-f}$ copies of
$C^{\otimes f}$ on fibres, $f=\lfloor k/m\rfloor$, grouped into $2^{k-f}/W^*$ recursive calls of the
$W^*$-role algorithm. It thus obeys $t(k)\le A+\frac{s}{W^*}t(\lfloor k/m\rfloor)$ for
$t(k)=T(k)/(W^*2^k)$.

\section{The grouped recursion}\label{sec:grouped}

\subsection{One edge is one tensor power}
For $M\in GL_m(\F)$ let $(P_Mg)(x)=g(M^{-1}x)$.

\begin{lemma}[batch identity]\label{lem:batch}
Let $z_1,\dots,z_\rho\in\F^m$ be linearly independent and let $M\in GL_m(\F)$ satisfy $Mz_j=e_j$
($1\le j\le\rho$). Then
\[
  \prod_{j=1}^\rho C_{z_j}=P_M^{-1}\big(C^{\otimes\rho}\otimes I_{2^{m-\rho}}\big)P_M .
\]
On $f$ columns, with $M$ applied in every column, $\big(\prod_jC_{z_j}\big)^{\otimes f}$ is a fixed
address permutation, followed by $2^{(m-\rho)f}$ independent copies of $C^{\otimes\rho f}$ on contiguous
fibres, followed by the inverse permutation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$(P_MR_zP_M^{-1}g)(x)=(P_M^{-1}g)(M^{-1}x+z)=g(x+Mz)$, so $P_MR_zP_M^{-1}=R_{Mz}$ and
$P_MC_zP_M^{-1}=C_{Mz}$. Hence $\prod_jC_{z_j}=P_M^{-1}\big(\prod_jC_{e_j}\big)P_M$, and
$C_{e_j}=aI+bR_{e_j}$ is $C$ on coordinate $j$ tensored with the identity. For the second statement
tensor over the $f$ columns, and order the addresses so that the $\rho f$ selected coordinates are the
lowest ones.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
If the $z_j$ are members $v_i$ ($i\in R$) of one orthonormal basis $v_1,\dots,v_m$ of $\F^m$, one may
take $Mx=(v_i\cdot x)_i$, reordered so that the coordinates in $R$ come last. Then $M^{-1}y=\sum_iy_iv_i$
by orthonormality.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[batch addressing]\label{lem:address}
Fix $m$ and $M\in GL_m(\F)$. On an array of length $2^{mf+r}$ indexed by $f$ columns and $r$ spectator
bits, the permutation of Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} and its inverse can be applied in $O(2^{mf+r})$
operations, with constants depending only on $m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the proof of \cite[Lemma~2.5]{oai}, with ``$\rho$ selected output bits per column'' in place of
one. Tabulate once, for every $m$-bit column digit $u$, the value $Mu$, split into its $\rho$ selected
bits and its $m-\rho$ remaining bits; the table has $2^m=O(1)$ entries. Traverse the digit prefix tree
of the address and add the two tabulated contributions per column, shifted to their output positions:
$O(1)$ work per node and fewer than $2^{mf+r+1}$ nodes. For the inverse, exchange source and
destination.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The RAM model of \cite{oailean} stores the $f$ bits of each coordinate as one word (transposed
digits). There the address map is computed coordinatewise as XOR-folds of words, in constant work per
address.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The grouped work function}
\begin{definition}[grouped work function]\label{def:gallot}
Let $m\ge3$ and $a\ge0$ be integers and $N:\{0,\dots,m-1\}\to\N$. Define $G:\N\to\N$ by
\[
  G(k)=
  \begin{cases}
  2^k+\displaystyle\sum_{c=0}^{m-1}N(c)\,2^{\,k-c\lfloor k/m\rfloor-a}\,G\big(c\lfloor k/m\rfloor\big)
    & \text{if }k\ge m(a+1),\\[1ex]
  2^k&\text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

This is well defined: for $k\ge m(a+1)$ and $c<m$, $c\lfloor k/m\rfloor\le(m-1)\lfloor k/m\rfloor<k$.
Moreover $k-c\lfloor k/m\rfloor\ge\lfloor k/m\rfloor\ge a+1$, so the exponent $k-c\lfloor k/m\rfloor-a$ is
a positive integer and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounds}
  2^{\,k-c\lfloor k/m\rfloor-a}\cdot2^a\cdot2^{\,c\lfloor k/m\rfloor}=2^k .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[supersolution]\label{lem:supersolution}
In Definition~\ref{def:gallot} suppose $N(0)=0$, and let $z\ge0$ satisfy
\[
  \Sigma:=\sum_{c=1}^{m-1}N(c)\Big(\frac cm\Big)^z<2^a .
\]
Then $G(k)\le K\,2^k(k+1)^z$ for all $k\ge0$, where $K=1+2^a/(2^a-\Sigma)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We show $G(k)\le K2^kk^z$ for $k\ge1$ by strong induction. If $k<m(a+1)$ then
$G(k)=2^k\le K2^kk^z$, since $K\ge1$ and $k^z\ge1$. Otherwise let $f=\lfloor k/m\rfloor\ge1$. For
$1\le c<m$ the child $cf$ satisfies $1\le cf<k$ and $(cf)^z\le(ck/m)^z=(c/m)^zk^z$. By induction and
\eqref{eq:rounds},
\[
  G(k)\le2^k+\sum_{c\ge1}N(c)\,2^{k-a}\,K\Big(\frac cm\Big)^zk^z
  =2^k\Big(1+K\frac{\Sigma}{2^a}k^z\Big)\le K2^kk^z ,
\]
where the last step uses $K(1-\Sigma/2^a)=(1-\Sigma/2^a)+1\ge1$ and $k^z\ge1$. Finally
$G(0)=1\le K$, and $k^z\le(k+1)^z$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The grouped tensor-power theorem}
\begin{theorem}[grouped tensor-power theorem]\label{thm:grouped}
Let an $(m,W^*)$-certificate be given with $m\ge3$, $W^*=2^a$, and edge multiset $\E$ with
$1\le\rho\le m-1$ for all $\rho\in\E$. Let $z\in[0,1]$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
  \sum_{\rho\in\E}\Big(\frac\rho m\Big)^z<W^* .
\end{equation}
Then there is a deterministic exact algorithm applying $C^{\otimes k}$ to an arbitrary complex array of
length $2^k$ in $O\big(2^k(k+1)^z\big)$ operations, for every $k\ge0$, in the model of
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We describe an algorithm $\mathcal A$ that transforms $W^*$ arrays of length $2^k$ simultaneously. One
array is handled by zero-filling the other $W^*-1$.

\emph{Base range.} If $k<K_0:=m(a+1)$, apply the $k$ factors of $C$ one at a time along their fibres,
in $O(W^*k2^k)=O(W^*2^k)$ operations, since $K_0$ is a constant.

\emph{Recursive step.} If $k\ge K_0$, write $k=mf+r$ with $f=\lfloor k/m\rfloor\ge a+1$ and
$0\le r<m$. Apply the $r$ leftover factors of $C$ directly, then execute the certificate on $f$ columns
with $r$ spectator bits. Gates and translations are pointwise and cost $O(W^*2^k)$ in total. An edge
move $(r,\tau,z_1,\dots,z_\rho)$ is executed by Lemma~\ref{lem:batch} and Lemma~\ref{lem:address}:
\begin{itemize}
\item permute the addresses of role $r$;
\item apply $C^{\otimes\rho f}$ to each of the $2^{k-\rho f}$ contiguous fibres;
\item permute back and translate by $\tau$.
\end{itemize}
Since $\rho\le m-1$, $k-\rho f\ge f\ge a$, so the fibres split into exactly $2^{k-\rho f-a}$ groups of
$W^*$. Each group is transformed by one recursive call of $\mathcal A$ at size $\rho f<k$. Correctness
follows from the certificate identity for $f$ columns, which holds for arbitrary contents of every
role, and from the correctness of the recursive calls.

\emph{Cost.} Everything except the recursive calls costs at most $A\cdot W^*2^k$ for a constant $A$,
as in \cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai}. With $N(c)$ the number of edge moves of size $c$ and $T(k)$ the cost of
$\mathcal A$, we get $T(k)\le AW^*2^k+\sum_cN(c)2^{k-cf-a}T(cf)$. By induction
$T(k)\le A'W^*G(k)$ with $G$ as in Definition~\ref{def:gallot}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:supersolution}
gives $T(k)=O(W^*2^k(k+1)^z)$.

\emph{Depth and workspace.} Along every recursion path
$k'=\rho\lfloor k/m\rfloor\le k-\lfloor k/m\rfloor\le k-1$. So consecutive array lengths at least halve,
and the workspace is $O(W^*2^k)$ as in \cite{oai}. The depth is
$O(\log(k/K_0)/\log\frac m{m-1})=O(\log k)$, so stacks and counters fit in $O(\log n)$-bit words.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:grouped}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:grouped} with $0<z\le1$, $F_n$ and exact convolution cost
$O\big(n(\log n)^z(\log\log n)^{4-z}\big)$, and hence $O(n(\log n)^{q})$ for every $q>z$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} and Corollary~\ref{cor:reduction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[grouping never hurts]\label{lem:ptheta}
Let $\E$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:grouped}, with $s=\sum_\rho\rho<W^*m$ and $|\E|>W^*$. Then
$F(z)=\sum_\rho(\rho/m)^z$ is continuous and strictly decreasing on $[0,1]$, with $F(0)>W^*>F(1)$.
Its unique root $p$ of $F(p)=W^*$ satisfies $p\le\theta=\log_m(s/W^*)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $0<\rho/m<1$. We have $F(0)=|\E|>W^*$ and $F(1)=s/m<W^*$. For $\rho\ge1$, $\rho^\theta\le\rho$, so
$F(\theta)\le m^{-\theta}s=W^*=F(p)$, and monotonicity gives $p\le\theta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[size of the gain]\label{rem:gain}
For $z=1-\varepsilon$ close to $1$, $(\rho/m)^{z}\approx(\rho/m)(1+\varepsilon\ln(m/\rho))$. Writing
$\Delta=W^*m-s$ for the saving,
\[
  1-\theta\approx\frac{\Delta}{W^*m\ln m},\qquad
  1-p\approx\frac{\Delta/m}{\sum_\rho\frac\rho m\ln\frac m\rho}.
\]
So $(1-p)/(1-\theta)$ is about $\ln m$ divided by the $\rho$-weighted mean of $\ln(m/\rho)$. It is large
when most of $s$ sits in edges of size close to $m$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Certifying the rate inequality}
\begin{lemma}[exponent certificate]\label{lem:certificate}
Let $m\ge2$, $L\ge\ln m$, $0<\varepsilon<1/L$ and $\mu=1/(1-\varepsilon L)$. For each $1\le c\le m$ let
$\ell_c\ge\ln(m/c)$. Then
\[
  \Big(\frac cm\Big)^{1-\varepsilon}\le\frac cm\big(1+\varepsilon\mu\ell_c\big).
\]
Consequently \eqref{eq:rate} holds for $z=1-\varepsilon$ whenever
$\sum_\rho\frac\rho m(1+\varepsilon\mu\ell_\rho)<W^*$, a finite inequality between rationals when
$\varepsilon$, $L$ and the $\ell_\rho$ are rational.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $y=\varepsilon\ln(m/c)\in[0,\varepsilon L]$. Then $(c/m)^{-\varepsilon}=e^y\le1/(1-y)=1+y/(1-y)\le
1+y/(1-\varepsilon L)\le1+\varepsilon\mu\ell_c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Upper bounds $\ell_c$ are obtained from $\ln2<0.6931471808$ together with $\ln y\le y-1$, or from
partial sums of the series of $\ln$.
\end{remark}

\section{OpenAI's formalized network}\label{sec:oainet}

OpenAI's formalization \cite{oailean} does not use the network of \cite[Section~2]{oai}. It uses a
smaller explicit one:
\begin{itemize}
\item three factors of $32$ binary coordinates each, so $m=32^3=32768$;
\item the $v=\binom{30}3=4060$ triples on $30$ of the coordinates of each factor;
\item $33$ centres;
\item one side wire for every ordered pair of triples;
\item $W^*=2^{50}$ roles.
\end{itemize}
Its certificate has $2^{50}m-\Delta$ directional moves with $\Delta=29\,406\,742\,400$,
and the formalization proves the per-direction exponent $1-2\cdot10^{-11}$ (the true value of
$1-\theta$ is $7.666\cdot10^{-11}$).

consecutive labels of a role differ by adding or removing a set of basis lines. Grouping each such

\begin{proposition}[rate]\label{prop:oairate}
For $\varepsilon=1.4657\cdot10^{-8}$, $\sum_cN(c)(c/m)^{1-\varepsilon}<2^{50}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate} with $L=16>\ln m=15\ln2$ and
$\ell_c=j_c\cdot0.6931471808+(m/(2^{j_c}c)-1)$, where $2^{j_c}\le m/c<2^{j_c+1}$. The resulting

(Section~\ref{sec:formal}). The exact root
of $F(p)=2^{50}$ is $1-p=1.4710\cdot10^{-8}$.
\end{proof}

By Propositions~\ref{prop:oaicert} and~\ref{prop:oairate}, Theorem~\ref{thm:grouped} applies with
$z=1-1.4657\cdot10^{-8}$. Corollary~\ref{cor:grouped} with $q=1-10^{-8}>z$ gives the bound. The
formal proof follows exactly this route (Section~\ref{sec:formal}).
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the Main Theorem}\label{sec:proofmain}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Theorem~\ref{col:thm:main}, the padded colour-register network with factor sizes $(30,20,41)$ and
the designs of Proposition~\ref{col:prop:designs} is a $(24600,2^{43})$-certificate whose edge sizes
lie in $[1,24599]$ and which satisfies \eqref{eq:rate} for $z=1-3.21\cdot10^{-6}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:grouped} gives $C^{\otimes k}$ in $O(2^k(k+1)^z)$, Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}
gives $O\big(n(\log n)^z(\log\log n)^{4-z}\big)$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:reduction} with
$q=1-3.2\cdot10^{-6}>z$ gives $O(n(\log n)^q)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Formal verification}\label{sec:formal}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Theorem~\ref{thm:formal} have been formally verified in the proof assistant
Lean~4 with the Mathlib library. The formal proof extends OpenAI's published Lean formalization
\cite{oailean} of \cite{oai}.

The formal statement is phrased in OpenAI's formal RAM model, with its operation-count semantics. It
asserts that there exist a program computing the root order $D$ from $n$ and a program computing
$F_nx$ exactly, for every $n\ge1$ and $x\in\C^n$, whose total work $W(n)$ satisfies OpenAI's original
bounds and
\[
  W(n)=O\!\left(n\,(\log n)^{1-3.2\cdot10^{-6}}\right),
\]
and whose integers, addresses and storage are polynomially bounded. The formal version of
Theorem~\ref{thm:formal} is the same statement with the exponent $1-10^{-8}$. Both statements are
also verified in their analogues for exact convolution.

The proof uses only the standard axioms of Lean's foundations (propositional extensionality, the axiom
of choice and the soundness of quotients) and no unverified computation. In particular, the finite
design data of Proposition~\ref{col:prop:designs}, about $1.04\cdot10^8$ ordered pairs, are checked by
the Lean kernel itself (Remark~\ref{col:rem:designs}).

OpenAI's definitions of the machine model and of correctness are used unchanged, and so is the rest
of their formal proof, in particular the reduction of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}, which the
kernel re-checks. Only three of OpenAI's files were modified, at the interface between the network and
the recursion, where the per-direction recursion is replaced by the grouped one. The new material
consists of the grouped recursion of Section~\ref{sec:grouped}, the colour-register network of
Section~\ref{sec:colour}, the designs, and the exponent inequality.

The formal proof differs from the written one in two respects. First, it certifies the rate
inequality \eqref{eq:rate} at $z=1-3.21\cdot10^{-6}$, through the bound of
Proposition~\ref{col:prop:exponent}(d), and not near the exact root $1-p=3.2791\cdot10^{-6}$.
Second, it obtains the slightly stronger bound $O\big(n(\log n)^z(\log\log n)^2\big)$, which implies
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} by Corollary~\ref{cor:reduction}.

What must be trusted is the Lean kernel, and the claim that OpenAI's formal model faithfully
expresses exact computation and its cost. The formal development is available from the author.

\section{Summary and outlook}\label{sec:summary}

We improved the exponent of OpenAI's exact power-saving DFT in two independent ways.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{grouped recursion} (Theorem~\ref{thm:grouped}) runs each edge of a network as one tensor
  power. It replaces $\log_m(\sum\rho_e/W^*)$ by the root of $\sum_e(\rho_e/m)^p=W^*$, never worse and
  typically much better. On OpenAI's formalized network it gives the bound
  $O\big(n(\log n)^{1-10^{-8}}\big)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:formal}), against the previously formalized
  $1-2\cdot10^{-11}$.
\item \emph{Colour registers} (Section~\ref{sec:colour}) replace the side wires of the paper's network
  by shared registers meeting at common labels. Combined with the grouped recursion they give
  $O\big(n(\log n)^{1-3.2\cdot10^{-6}}\big)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), seven orders of magnitude beyond
  the original gap of $2.1\cdot10^{-13}$.
\end{enumerate}
Both results have been formally verified (Section~\ref{sec:formal}).

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Better register designs.} The searched coordinate designs are about three times above a
  simple counting lower bound. Designs in which each element needs $O(1)$ registers (``flag-meeting''
  designs) would roughly triple $1-p$. We do not know whether they exist.
\item \emph{Lorentzian families.} Replacing triples by the Lorentzian vector families of our earlier
  analysis gives $1-p\ge2.479\cdot10^{-7}$ with side wires. Combining them with colour registers
  requires meeting labels for pair-type vectors, which we have not constructed.
\item \emph{Limits of the method.} Within the 12-step register pattern, the stage-2 auxiliaries
  dominate the cost, because their labels must contain $B\otimes D_2$. A different invocation pattern
  would be needed to go substantially further.
\end{itemize}
